            How MarbleRush works

            1. Back a marble. Every stake goes into one pool. Odds are what the pool would pay right now, and they move as money arrives, exactly like a tote board.
            2. Ready up. When everyone at the table is ready the lights go out. A 30 second clock runs the lobby: anyone without a bet when it expires is staked the 5 minimum on the least backed marble.
            3. Every course is new. Built for each race from a library of over a hundred sections, about 3 to 4 minutes long, and arranged so the race stays open to the end: one of two fair openers, calm sections early, a mix of machines in the run-in, and a Mempool that bunches the field.
            4. Machines wear. The first marble to reach a machine meets it at full strength: the longest flipper, the longest freeze, the longest wait at a laser or a cup, the strongest pull of a whale's gravity well. Each marble after meets it a little weaker. Nothing knows who is leading; being first is simply what makes you meet it first.
            5. Chaos is part of the course. Sweeping white and grey holes warp a marble about 3 seconds; cryo traps freeze for 2; bridges, vaults, claws and reorgs hold or send back a moment. None of it knows who is leading.
            6. Knockouts are rare. The Jump, the Edge, the Breach or a black hole can take a marble out, at most two a race, so both paid places always go to finishers.
            7. Top two are paid. 75% of the pool to the winner's backers, 25% to the runner-up's, each split in proportion to stake.
            8. Provably fair. The race is decided before betting opens. Its seed's SHA-256 hash is shown while you bet, and the seed is revealed at the lights so anyone can replay the race.

            A parimutuel pool pays shares of a fixed pot rather than fixed multiples, so a heavily backed marble pays less even when it wins. Rake is 5%. All money here is demo money.
